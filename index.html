Meet Craig

A farmer who
learned to fly.

Craig McPherson is a fifth-generation Texas dairy farmer and rancher. He and his wife Cathie live on a family-owned farm in Blum, TX. Craig dreamed of flying since he was a child, but the time and expense of a general aviation license kept it out of reach — until he spotted an article in a farming magazine about powered parachutes (PPCs).

He visited an instructor in Oklahoma, and even though it was too windy to fly that day, he was hooked. He came home and bought a small, used PPC. That was in 1994. Over the years he worked his way up to the top-of-the-line Airwolf 912 and integrated flying into farming and ranching.

Craig enjoys sharing his love of flying so much that he founded Blue Skies Gyros and became a dealer for Powrachute, and later AutoGyro. Becoming a Certified Flight Instructor has been his most enjoyable accomplishment — over 3,000 hours instructing students in gyros and over 2,000 in PPCs. His students have included seven-time PRCA World Champion Ty Murray; Ty's wife, singer/songwriter Jewel; NASCAR driver Carl Edwards; and country singer Blake Shelton have also flown with Craig.

“I love witnessing the excitement and pure joy people express the first time I take them up. I also love watching my students progress into capable and safe pilots. Being a CFI and pilot has been such a blessing in my life and has given me such amazing opportunities.”— Craig McPherson

Led by Craig, our team brings over 27 years of aviation experience to help you achieve your flying dreams. We are passionate about gyroplanes and dedicated to providing the highest quality instruction and service — our friendly and knowledgeable staff is here to support you every step of the way, from your first flight to owning your own aircraft.

FAA requirements — sport pilot license

  • Be 17 years old or older (16 to start training / student certificate)
  • Be able to read, write, and understand English
  • Hold a current U.S. driver license (or 3rd class medical)
  • Pass an FAA sport pilot knowledge written test
  • Complete a minimum of 20 hours of training in a gyroplane
  • Pass an FAA sport pilot flight checkride
  • Student Pilot Certificate from any FSDO or Designated Pilot Examiner

The hours, broken down

Flight training with an instructor15 hours minimum
Checkride prep (within 60 days of test)3 hours
Solo flight training5 hours minimum
Solo cross-country2 hours · one solo cross-country flight of at least 50 nm total distance, with full-stop landings at a minimum of two points, and one segment of at least 25 nm straight-line between takeoff and landing
Takeoffs & landings to a full stop10 — each landing involving a flight in the traffic pattern, at an airport

What you will learn

Ground and flight training from an authorized CFI on the FAA areas of operation (FAR 61.311):

  • Preflight preparation & preflight procedures
  • Airport, seaplane base, and gliderport operations, as applicable
  • Takeoffs (or launches), landings, and go-arounds
  • Performance maneuvers, and for gliders, performance speeds
  • Ground reference maneuvers (not applicable to gliders and balloons)
  • Navigation
  • Emergency operations
  • Post-flight procedures

How do I become a pilot?

All first-time LSA pilots are required to be trained by a qualified FAA Certified Flight Instructor (CFI). You will need to obtain a Student Pilot Certificate from any FAA Flight Standards District Office (FSDO) or from a Designated Pilot Examiner (DPE). To get started you must be 16 years of age, possess a valid state-issued driver license or photo ID (or a 3rd class airman medical certificate), and be able to read, speak and write the English language.

Who can recommend me for the practical exam?

Only your Certified Flight Instructor — with a logbook endorsement that you've met the requirements of Sections 61.309, 61.311, and 61.313.

When should I take the knowledge exam?

It's recommended after your solo cross-country and before your CFI signs off — but start studying from the very first lesson.

Gyroplane information

What is a
gyroplane?

Also known as gyrocopters, autogyros or rotaplanes, a gyroplane uses an unpowered rotor in autorotation for lift and an engine-driven propeller for thrust. It will not stall or spin — and in an engine failure, the pilot glides down and lands gently, under full control. While similar to a helicopter rotor in appearance, the gyroplane's rotor must have air flowing through the rotor disc to generate rotation.

Gyroplane facts

  • Amazingly stable — turbulence and wind have little or no effect on gyroplanes, so there's no bouncing around.
  • Minimal takeoff distances, typically 30–300 feet depending on weather conditions and load.
  • Very short landing distances, typically 0–30 feet.
  • Wide range of speed, including very slow speeds safely possible.
  • Gyroplanes cost about 10% of a helicopter to own, yet can accomplish about 90% of what a helicopter can do.
  • Unlike a helicopter, gyroplanes cannot hover or take off and land vertically.
  • Our gyroplanes will not stall or spin. In the unlikely event of an engine failure, the pilot can easily glide down and make a gentle landing under full control.

Safety

Safety is our first priority when selecting and building gyros. Our gyroplanes undergo Airworthiness Certifications and we work closely with the FAA to ensure the highest compliance standards. Amazingly stable — turbulence and wind have little or no effect, so there's no bouncing around.

Performance

Minimal takeoff distances (typically 30–300 ft) and very short landings (0–30 ft), with a wide speed range including safe very slow flight. A gyroplane costs about 10% of a helicopter to own, yet can do about 90% of what a helicopter can — it just can't hover or take off vertically.

Aesthetics

Appearances make a huge impression, so we focus on gyroplanes with eye-catching designs that do not sacrifice the safety of the aircraft. Cost-to-power is a problem for any plane — we provide an array of options to suit your needs and performance output.

How long does it take?

Most gyros can be built in about two to four weeks, depending on the model.

How much does it cost?

Depending on the model, builder-assisted assembly costs from $3,000 to $5,000.

How soon can I get a kit?

Anywhere from 12 weeks or longer, depending on your model and factory schedules.

Is it a kit?

Yes. In the USA gyroplanes are still considered experimental aircraft, although AutoGyro machines are certified and sold fully assembled in Europe and most other countries. Over 2,000 AutoGyro aircraft are flying worldwide.

Is it difficult to build?

No — but AutoGyro requires you to participate in the Builder Assist Program with one of our technicians. Complicated machining and fabrication are done at the factory; you'll drill some holes and assemble enough parts to meet the 51% builder rule for amateur-built aircraft.

What about spare parts?

Our Maryland headquarters location carries most parts that we don't typically stock.

How much does it cost to own and maintain a gyroplane?

Budget $30 for every hour you fly to cover all operating costs — fuel, 100-hour condition inspections, oil changes, rotor replacement ($4,000 at 2,600 hours) and Rotax engine TBO (at 2,000 hours).

If I have an aircraft, can you service it?

Yes — Blue Skies Gyros offers a complete range of FAA and LSA certified service for your light sport aircraft, including 100-hour condition inspections, light sport aircraft repairs, and maintenance.

How do I become an FAA certified repairman for my gyro?

Build it yourself, or participate in AutoGyro's Builder Assist Program — which we strongly recommend, because it ensures a safe and properly built aircraft. We are certified for light sport fixed wing, powered parachutes, trikes, and gyroplanes.
